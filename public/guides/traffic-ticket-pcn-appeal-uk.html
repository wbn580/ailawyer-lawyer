How to appeal a parking ticket or PCN (UK)

Plain-language guide · reviewed for general accuracy · United Kingdom

Finding a yellow envelope stuck to your windscreen is never a good moment. But before you pay up, it's worth knowing that many parking tickets and Penalty Charge Notices (PCNs) can be successfully challenged — and the appeals system is free. Here's how the process works in England and Wales.

1. Know which type of ticket you have

This is the critical first step because the appeal route is different. A Penalty Charge Notice (PCN) is issued by the council or Transport for London on public roads — for parking contraventions, bus lane violations, or moving traffic offences like yellow box junctions. A Fixed Penalty Notice (FPN) is issued by the police for more serious driving offences. A private parking charge notice is from a parking company on private land (supermarket car park, retail park, residential estate) and is a civil matter under contract law. This guide focuses on council PCNs and private tickets.

2. Council PCN: the three-stage challenge process

Stage one is an informal challenge — write to the council within 14 days (or the "discount period") explaining why you think the PCN should be cancelled. Common grounds include: the signage was unclear or missing, you had a valid ticket or permit displayed, your vehicle broke down, you were loading/unloading and the rules allow it, or the contravention didn't happen (you can ask for photographic evidence). Stage two: if the council rejects your informal challenge, they issue a "Notice to Owner" and you can make a formal representation within 28 days. Stage three: if that is rejected, you can appeal to the independent adjudicator — the Traffic Penalty Tribunal (outside London) or London Tribunals (within London).

Tip: if you pay within 14 days, most councils offer a 50% discount. Making an informal challenge usually pauses the discount clock — check the council's policy. If your challenge fails, you can still pay the discounted amount if you act fast.

3. Private parking tickets: contract law, not crime

Private parking charges are invoices for an alleged breach of contract — not fines. The parking company relies on implied contract (signage at the car park entrance) and the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 to pursue the registered keeper. If you receive one, you can appeal to the parking company first, citing grounds like unclear signage, the charge being disproportionate (a penalty rather than a genuine pre-estimate of loss), or that you were not the driver. If rejected, escalate to the independent appeals service: POPLA (Parking on Private Land Appeals) for BPA members, or the IAS (Independent Appeals Service) for IPC members. If you lose at POPLA/IAS, you can still choose to pay, or wait to see if the company issues a county court claim.

4. Don't ignore it

Ignoring a council PCN leads to a Charge Certificate (50% more), then an Order for Recovery, and potentially bailiff action. For private tickets, ignoring them used to be common advice, but since the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 the parking company can pursue the registered keeper even if they don't know who was driving. While many companies don't issue court claims, they can — and a County Court Judgment (CCJ) affects your credit rating.

Common questions

PCN vs private ticket — what's the difference?
A PCN is from the council on public roads. Private tickets are on private land and governed by contract law.
Should I pay the 50% discount or appeal?
If you have strong grounds, appeal. If the grounds are weak, the discounted rate might be the safer bet.
How do I appeal a private parking ticket?
Challenge the parking company first, then escalate to POPLA or IAS — both free and independent.
